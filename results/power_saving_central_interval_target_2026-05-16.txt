Power-saving central-interval target
Date: 2026-05-16

Artifact:
  notes/28_power_saving_central_interval_target.md

Mathematical advance:
  Replaced the vague "central interval theorem" with a sharper conductor-growth
  target.

Key identity:
  For a frontier E and finite seed conductor c(E),

    K(E) = C(E) - 1 - H(E) = kappa(A,k) + 2 c(E) + 1.

  Thus the tail obstruction is controlled exactly by finite seed conductor
  growth, up to a fixed constant.

Strict case target:
  c(E) = o(T(E)), where T(E) is the minimum frontier term.

Exact-critical target:
  c(E) = O(T(E)^(1-epsilon)) for some epsilon > 0.

Reason:
  Strict slack dominates sublinear conductor growth.  In the exact-critical
  case, a power-saving conductor bound turns near-collision failures into
  S-unit/Subspace-Theorem forbidden approximations.

Status:
  This does not prove Erdős 124.  It gives a concrete noncomputational theorem
  target that would finish the qualitative proof within the current
  architecture.

Audit effect:
  The qualitative global audit now has one open mathematical bottleneck:
  prove the power-saving central conductor theorem.  Residue saturation and
  explicit Baker bounds are retained as optional/effective routes, not as
  separate blockers for the qualitative proof.
